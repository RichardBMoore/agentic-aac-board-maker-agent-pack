# Teacher notes - QCIA Community Access: Going to the Shops

Fixture: `qcia-community-shops`

## Purpose

A community access AAC board that supports practical shopping communication, safety/help language, and teacher evidence notes for QCIA-style participation.

## How it works

- **Shop talk** (3x3): How I talk, Hello, Stop, ABC, Where is it?, Choosing ▶, Help, Wait, Goodbye.
- **Choosing** (3x3): I want to buy this, Too expensive, Stop, ◀ Shop talk, Yes, At the counter ▶, Help, Wait, No.
- **At the counter** (3x3): Ready to pay, That's all, Thank you, ◀ Choosing, Yes, Core words ▶, Help, Wait, No.

The house standards tool added a **Core words** page (I, want, don't, like, go, more) that builds messages in the message bar, a **Word endings** page (-s, -ing, -ed, with Undo and Start again) and an **ABC keyboard** (QWERTY letter groups, then letters; Speak, Delete and Help on the keyboard page). Talking pages link forward with the right-middle button and back with the left-middle button; ABC sits left-middle on the first page.

## Partner card (summary)

- Model this week: Where is it?, I want to buy this, Ready to pay, Thank you.
- Wait at least 15 seconds after you model, comment or ask.
- Comment more than you question, for example: Touch Where is it?: "I can't see the bread. Where is it?"
- If more support is needed, go from least to most help and never move the student's hand (no hand-over-hand).

Print `partner-card.html` for everyone who talks with the student.

## Notes

### Modelling

Model real community phrases before the trip and during role play: greeting, asking where something is, choosing, answering Yes or No, paying, and saying Stop or Wait.

### Customisation

Replace shop and item vocabulary with the actual excursion, class enterprise or role-play context. Help, Stop, Wait, Yes and No keep the same cells on every shop page; keep them there when you change the topic words.

### Privacy

Use de-identified notes only. Do not record names, behaviour details or community incidents inside the generated files.

### Safety

This supports communication during community access; it does not replace adult supervision, risk assessment or emergency processes.

## Evidence

QCIA evidence can note participation in a familiar community routine, functional communication attempts, help-seeking, choice-making and safety language.

Adult safety support and access prompts are recorded as context, not as reduced QCIA achievement.
