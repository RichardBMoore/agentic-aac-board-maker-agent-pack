# Curriculum And QCIA Translation

The agent's job is to preserve learning intent while reducing access barriers. Do not water down curriculum by default. Translate curriculum into accessible communication moves.

## Translation Pattern

For any curriculum task:

1. Identify the cognitive demand: identify, choose, sequence, compare, explain, justify, evaluate, create, reflect.
2. Identify the communication functions needed.
3. Reduce motor/language access load without removing the thinking.
4. Provide AAC pathways: core words, sentence starters, topic vocabulary, repair/help.
5. Provide evidence options: chosen answer, assembled sentence, recorded/saved response, teacher observation note, print/export.

## Cognitive Demand To AAC Moves

- **Identify/name:** choose from field; match; say what it is.
- **Describe:** choose features; use adjectives; combine noun + descriptor.
- **Sequence:** first, next, then, last, before, after.
- **Compare:** same, different, more, less, bigger, smaller, like/unlike.
- **Explain:** because, reason, evidence, I think, this shows.
- **Evaluate/opinion:** I like, I don't like, best, important, fair/unfair, safe/unsafe.
- **Predict:** I think, next, maybe, will, because.
- **Reflect:** I did, hard/easy, I need, next time, proud, help.
- **Ask/question:** who, what, where, when, why, how, show me, repeat.

## Australian Curriculum Participation Boards

A board should let the student participate in the same lesson discourse, not just identify pictures.

### English

Functions: retell, character, setting, problem, opinion, because, evidence, question, audience.

Useful buttons: I think, because, character, setting, problem, important, funny, sad, ask, retell, next, finished.

### Mathematics

Functions: count, compare, pattern, choose strategy, explain reasoning.

Useful buttons: more, less, same, different, count, add, take away, pattern, how many, I think, because, check.

### Science

Functions: observe, predict, classify, record, explain change/cause.

Useful buttons: look, observe, predict, change, living, non-living, test, record, same, different, because.

### HASS

Functions: time/place/community/rules/perspectives.

Useful buttons: past, now, place, map, community, rule, fair, important, people, because, question.

### HPE

Functions: safety, feelings, relationships, movement, choices, respect.

Useful buttons: safe, unsafe, healthy, feelings, team, respect, help, stop, my turn, choice, because.

### Technologies

Functions: design, make, test, improve, safety, problem solving.

Useful buttons: plan, make, test, improve, problem, solution, safe, digital, because, next.

### The Arts

Functions: make, perform, respond, describe, express preference.

Useful buttons: look, listen, make, perform, colour, sound, movement, I like, I feel, because.

## QCIA Translation

QCIA curriculum plans and Statements of Achievement report against QCAA's five curriculum organisers (QCE & QCIA policy and procedures handbook, section 3.2). Use the official organiser names below so generated evidence notes map straight onto the plan.

QCIA evidence often benefits from practical, observable communication.

### Personal and living dimensions (PLD)

Functions: sequence, request help, indicate safety, reflect.

Buttons: first, next, finished, help, safe, hot, sharp, wash, cook, clean, I need, because.

### Community, citizenship and the environment (CCE)

Functions: choose destination/action, ask for help, identify safety, money/transport, reflect.

Buttons: go, stop, help, safe, shop, bus, money, where, wait, finished, I need, thank you.

### Leisure and recreation (LR)

Functions: choose, request more/again, express preference, turn-taking.

Buttons: I choose, like, don't like, again, more, my turn, your turn, music, game, outside, finished.

### Vocational and transition activities (VTA)

Functions: task sequence, ask, report completion, self-advocate.

Buttons: work, break, first, next, done, help, too hard, I can, I need, job, plan.

### Communication and technologies (CT)

Functions: greet, comment, ask, repair, choose, refuse.

Buttons: hello, my name, question, I think, I like, help, repeat, wait, no, yes, finished.

## Assessment Evidence Without Over-Scaffolding

Avoid making every answer identical. Include:

- own-choice/editable slots;
- multiple plausible reasons;
- student opinion buttons;
- teacher observation field;
- print/export of selected sequence or final sentence;
- opportunities for modelling and repeated attempts.

## Teacher Evidence Note Template

```text
Evidence focus: <curriculum/QCIA goal>
Student communication opportunities: <functions>
Access method: <method>
Teacher can observe: selections, sequence, chosen reason/opinion, help/repair use, participation in shared task.
This support reduces access load while preserving: <learning intent>.
```
