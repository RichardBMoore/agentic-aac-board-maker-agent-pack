## AAC Practice

- ASHA describes AAC as communication support that supplements or compensates for speech/language impairments and includes multiple techniques such as gestures, tangible objects, picture boards, letter boards, and speech-generating devices.
- ASHA notes that AAC systems should be customizable for the user's icons, vocabulary, languages, and dialects.
- ASHA also discusses misconceptions around AAC and notes that AAC may support natural speech when used multimodally.
- Source: https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/professional-issues/augmentative-and-alternative-communication/

## Communication Access

- ISAAC frames communication access around understanding, being understood, preferred communication methods, time and opportunity to communicate, accessible information, and support from a trusted person when needed.
- Source: https://isaac-online.org/english/communication-access/

## Differentiation, UDL, And SETT

- CAST UDL frames design around multiple means of engagement, representation, and action/expression. For AAC boards, this means the student should have more than one way to participate and show learning.
- The SETT Framework keeps assistive technology decisions grounded in Student, Environment, Task, and Tools rather than picking software first.
- Australian Curriculum student diversity guidance and the Disability Standards for Education support reasonable adjustments so students can access, participate, and demonstrate learning.
- QCAA QCIA evidence should show participation and achievement in planned learning while keeping access supports visible as context rather than curriculum judgement.
- Sources:
  - https://udlguidelines.cast.org/
  - https://www.joyzabala.com/
  - https://www.australiancurriculum.edu.au/resources/student-diversity/students-with-disability/
  - https://www.education.gov.au/disability-standards-education-2005
  - https://www.qcaa.qld.edu.au/senior/certificates-and-qualifications/qcia

## Web Accessibility

- WCAG 2.2 target-size minimum is 24 by 24 CSS pixels or sufficient spacing, but classroom AAC and eye-gaze targets normally need to be much larger than this minimum.
- WCAG pointer cancellation supports avoiding accidental activation by not firing on pointer-down, allowing abort/undo, or using up-event activation.
- Sources:
  - https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum
  - https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pointer-cancellation

## Access Methods

- Tobii Dynavox describes common high-tech AAC access methods as touch, eye gaze, mouse dwell, and scanning, and notes these should be customised to individual needs.
- Eye gaze controls a computer with the eyes. Mouse dwell holds the cursor over an item for a set time. Scanning highlights options and the user selects with a switch, keyboard, or mouse.
- Tobii Dynavox also documents a "look away before reselecting" setting to reduce repeated accidental selections; implement the same idea with a leave/guard requirement where useful.
- Sources:
  - https://www.tobiidynavox.com/pages/access-methods-for-aac
  - https://us.tobiidynavox.com/blogs/support-articles/how-to-prevent-accidental-selections-with-eye-gaze-in-td-snap-1

## PRC-Saltillo Device Safety

- PRC-Saltillo documentation states that the software is not intended for emergency calls or as a sole communication aid.
- PRC-Saltillo eye-tracking safety guidance highlights frequent breaks, eye strain/fatigue, positioning advice from vision providers, and attention to possible medical or seizure risks.
- Device specs vary by model and year. Confirm the exact target device rather than hardcoding screen dimensions.
- Source: https://documentation.prc-saltillo.com/docs/accent-1400-30-specifications
