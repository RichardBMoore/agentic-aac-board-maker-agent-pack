# Scaffold Design Patterns From Richard's Resources

Use this reference when building, reviewing, or adapting English/curriculum scaffolds for Richard.

## At-level scaffolds

Richard distinguishes at-level support from ICP/adapted-level work. Do **not** assume that a scaffold is modified curriculum just because it is highly supportive.

Core principle:

> Scaffold the route, not the rigour.

At-level scaffolds should:

- keep the original curriculum/task expectation intact;
- reveal the hidden structure of the task;
- reduce blank-page anxiety and executive load;
- preserve student voice and independent thinking;
- chunk the task into meaningful sections aligned to the actual assessment sequence;
- name the purpose of each section;
- include sentence starters that open thinking but do not write the whole response;
- include models/examples, glossary/metalanguage, and self-review checklists;
- connect sections to marking-guide/success-criteria language at point of need;
- include practical access supports such as TTS, dictation/STT where useful, autosave, print/export, focus mode, and high contrast.

Example pattern from Richard's Essential English/music-vlog scaffolds:

1. Genre/role first: student writes as a vlogger, reviewer, article writer, analyst, etc.
2. Whole-task decomposition: hook, context, analysis, values/attitudes/beliefs, multimodal/textual evidence, conclusion, final checklist.
3. Assessment language embedded beside the writing task.
4. Metalanguage kept visible: representation, audience, purpose, genre, effect, text structures, language features, attitudes/values/beliefs.
5. Export/print/draft workflow built in.

Pitfall: Do not call these ICP or simplified resources unless Richard has said they are ICP. They are often at-level scaffolds valued by mainstream/at-level learners.

## ICP/AAC scaffolds

Core principle:

> Translate the assessment into accessible communication.

ICP/AAC scaffolds differ from at-level scaffolds. They deliberately adjust curriculum level and response/access mode while keeping the student connected to the shared classroom task and producing defensible evidence.

When building similar resources:

1. Confirm ICP level, original class task, assessment mode, and required evidence.
2. Identify the exact communication functions the student must perform: choose, sequence, express opinion, explain because, ask, answer, rehearse, record, reflect.
3. Convert abstract task requirements into concrete AAC/sentence-builder moves.
4. Use symbol/core-word banks where relevant, not only text boxes.
5. Provide public/example content to reduce research load when research is not the core assessment demand.
6. Build in rehearsal and playback, not just final submission.
7. Include teacher-visible evidence: script, recording, exported notes/report, observation prompts, judgement guide.
8. Preserve agency by allowing an own-choice option and editable text where practical.
9. Separate student-facing calm workflow from teacher-facing assessment/reporting.

Example pattern from Richard's Year 7 ICP/AAC hero podcast scaffolds:

- Level 3: choose/name hero; share 3-5 facts in sequence; express opinion with reason; express appreciation/admiration; ask 1-2 questions; build script; record/export.
- Level 4: add hook, 4-6 events, fact/opinion distinction, why-it-matters explanation, literary device, two questions, conclusion, audience engagement.
- AAC builder: large symbol/text tiles, core words (I, like, think, want, is, not, because, and, but, more), hero fact banks, opinion starters, quick responses, Q&A support, script/practice/record/report, break/reset support, clear-data confirmation.

Pitfall: Purpose-built specificity is not a weakness for ICP/AAC supports. If specificity creates access for the student, it is a feature.
